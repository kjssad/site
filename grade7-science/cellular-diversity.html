Competency and Objectives

Competency (LC3): Recognize that some organisms consist of a single cell (unicellular), such as bacteria, and some of many cells (multicellular), such as humans.
  • LO6 Classify organisms as unicellular or multicellular by number of cells.
  • LO7 Enumerate examples of unicellular and multicellular organisms.
  • LO8 Distinguish prokaryotic from eukaryotic cells by their characteristics.
Essential Question: How does the number of cells that make up an organism affect its structure, function, and how it lives and survives?

Research task · 8 min

Source reading: Unicellular vs. Multicellular

Source: Science 7 textbook, pp. 78–79 (or the article your teacher gives).
  • Unicellular organisms (bacteria, archaeans, protists, yeasts) do everything they need inside one cell.
  • Multicellular organisms (humans, plants, animals, fungi) are made of more than one cell. Cells have different functions and work together.
  • Groups of cells specialize in specific tasks.

Your job: Write 3 facts in your own words (no copying!) and the title of your source.

Analogy

One-room store vs. a mall

🏪 Unicellular

One person runs the whole store alone: selling, cleaning, stocking, and cashiering.

🏬 Multicellular

A big mall: different workers have different jobs, and they work together.

Cell Detective · click each clue

Is an amoeba one cell or many?

Amoeba
Onion skin (many cells)
Onion: yes, many box shapes separated by walls, and each box is one cell. Amoeba: no lines inside. It is one continuous body.
Onion: each box has its own nucleus. Amoeba: one nucleus for the whole body (a typical Amoeba proteus).
Pseudopods (“false feet”) are bulges of the SAME cell. They stretch out and pull back. They are not separate cells.
Yes, one membrane wraps the whole amoeba. Verdict: one cell, so it is unicellular.

Detective rule: boxes with walls = many cells. One continuous body inside one outline = one cell.

Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic: Compare

ProkaryoticEukaryotic
NucleusNo (DNA in nucleoid)Yes
OrganellesNo membrane-bound organellesHas membrane-bound organelles
SizeVery smallLarger, more complex
ExamplesBacteria, archaeansPlants, animals, fungi, protists
ExtraCell wall; may have flagellum, pilusPlant cells: cellulose cell wall
Link the ideas + misconception check
Unicellular organisms are often prokaryotic; multicellular organisms are typically eukaryotic.

Careful!

  • “Small means unicellular.” ✗ A tiny seedling is still multicellular.
  • “All unicellular organisms are prokaryotes.” ✗ Yeast and Paramecium are unicellular but eukaryotic.
